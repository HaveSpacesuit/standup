What the Sprint view is for, and where it drifts

Intent to preserve

The People view answers “who is doing what?”. The Sprint view should answer three different questions: How far along is the sprint? What moved, and when? What changed in scope since we committed? Every proposal keeps those questions. What changes is the presentation, so switching views feels like changing the lens on one board, not opening another app.

Where it's out of sync today

  • Two card designs. Sprint still uses the legacy card (state pill, “Effort 8” footer, full owner name). People uses the quieter Development card.
  • Two header styles. Here: tinted full-height columns, a large colored count, and “Current sprint effort: N”. People: neutral headers with a count pill and “N pts”.
  • Settings stop at the view switch. Compact density and the Needs attention bar don't exist here, so blocked or failing work is easier to miss in the view meant to show sprint health.
  • The 175px “Sprint” column mostly repeats the sprint name the ribbon already shows.
  • Staircase layout. One card per row means 25 items take 25 rows. Most of the canvas is empty, and you can't scan a single column without scrolling.
  • Move arrows say little. They show only the farthest column visited: not when, not for how long, not how many moves. They're aria-hidden and have no legend.
  • Scope change is invisible. Mid-sprint additions look identical to committed work, and removed items silently disappear, even though the forecast chip counts both.
  • “Progress through the sprint” lives only in a 120×28 ribbon chart. The board itself only ever shows today.
Proposals

1One board, two lensesM

Same board as People. The view switch changes how lanes are grouped (by person or by sprint), not the design language.

What changes

  • Same grid, column headers (count pill + “N pts”), calm canvas (only a non-empty Blocked cell is tinted), cards, density setting, and Needs attention bar as People.
  • Cards pack into cells instead of a staircase, so about 25 items fit on one screen.
  • The 175px Sprint column becomes the 208px lane header used in People: a status bar, items · pts, and flags. Other sprints become collapsed lanes.
  • The Development card gets an owner avatar beside the effort pill (name in a StrataKit tooltip), since lanes here aren't people.

Why

One mental model, and every future improvement to the People view lands here for free.

StrataKit & data

No new data. Let KanbanBoardGrid take lanes as input (people or sprint groups), move AttentionBar up from KanbanBoard to the page, and add a showOwner prop to DevelopmentCard.

Watch-outs

Packing removes the per-row arrows, so ship it together with 2 to keep the “what moved” story.

2Journey trailsM

Replace arrows with a tiny, honest timeline on every card.

What changes

  • The bottom edge of each card becomes a 4px timeline on a shared scale from Day 0 to today, colored by the column the item was in each day. A dotted lead marks an item added late.
  • One caption in the meta row replaces the aging dots: “7d in Active”, “Back from Review · 2d”, or “Done Oct 30”.
  • Hover or focus shows a dashed “Started here” ghost in the starting column, joined to the card by a curved connector.
  • Needs attention gains a Moved back chip.

Why

Answers “what moved, and when?” for every card at a glance. Works in packed cells, and is accessible: role="img" with a text journey.

Data

useEffortFlowHistory already builds a per-item TimelineEntry list. Expose the status spans instead of only visitedStatusesByItemId.

Watch-outs

Trail colors must match the column bars exactly. The ghost appears only on hover or focus; it's never a permanent overlay.

3Commitment lanesM

Make scope change a first-class part of the board.

What changes

  • Lanes become Committed at start, Added during sprint, and Removed from sprint. Removed is collapsed by default and holds dashed ghost cards with struck-through effort and “Moved to Sprint 43 · Oct 29”.
  • Lane flags make scope visible: “3 from Sprint 41”, “Scope +12%”.
  • The next sprint stays a collapsed lane at the bottom.

Why

Uses the same committed / added / removed math as the forecast, so the chip and the board finally tell the same story. Sprint reviews can point at the board instead of a spreadsheet.

Data

Already loaded: useSprintScopeChanges (startItemIds, removedItems) and rolledOverItemIds.

Watch-outs

An item that moves between iterations several times needs one rule. Suggest: its first entry decides “committed” vs “added”.

4Flow headersS

Put sprint progress where people already look.

What changes

  • Each column header gets a 64×18 sparkline of its points across the sprint, plus the change since start, colored by meaning: Done ▲ is positive, Blocked ▲ is critical.
  • Active and Review show their oldest item (“oldest 9d”).
  • Sparklines hide when the board is narrower than about 1100px.

Why

“Is Done growing? Is Review piling up?” becomes visible without opening the ribbon chart.

Data

effortFlowPoints from useEffortFlowHistory; the oldest age comes from the same timelines.

5Sprint replayL

Let the board show the sprint's story, not just today.

What changes

  • A slim timeline strip under the ribbon: cumulative flow across all 15 days, future days hatched, the forecast as a dashed line, and a playhead.
  • Drag the slider, step with ◀ ▶, or press Play. Cards animate to where they stood at the end of each day (FLIP; honors reduced motion).
  • On a past day, a banner offers “Back to today”, and the attention bar and live details (PRs, next steps) are hidden.
  • In the Sprint view, the toolbar's primary action becomes Replay sprint, mirroring Start standup in People.

Why

Lets you tell the sprint's story in a retro or review in about 30 seconds.

StrataKit & data

The scrubber is a MUI Slider, which StrataKit already themes. The timeline chart is a deliberate extension beyond StrataKit. Status per day already exists via statusAsOf. The exact add or remove day needs System.IterationPath parsed from revisions already fetched, and removed items may need one extra revisions query.

Watch-outs

Animation cost on large boards: animate only visible cards, and skip animation when reduced motion is on.
